# Alternative itinerary: New York first, then Acadia

**Direction:** Seattle → New York → Maine/Acadia → Seattle  
**Research date:** August 23, 2026  
**Priorities applied:** three full New York days, five full Acadia days, nonstop flights where available, EWR preferred over JFK, Alaska preferred when practical, and a round-trip Maine rental returned to its pickup airport.

## The calendar constraint

Three genuinely full days in New York plus five genuinely full days in Acadia require **eight destination days**, plus a Seattle→New York travel day, a New York→Maine transfer day, and a Maine→Seattle travel day. That is **11 calendar days**. The original October 9–18 window contains only 10 calendar days.

The clean itinerary therefore returns **Monday, October 19**, one day beyond the original window. A compressed version can return October 18, but either the third New York day or one Acadia day becomes a partial day. Both versions are below so that the tradeoff is explicit.

### Why Bangor is the recommended Maine airport

- **EWR–BGR is nonstop on United** and operates multiple times on many days; current published schedules range from morning to late evening. [Current EWR–BGR schedule](https://www.directflights.com/EWR-BGR)
- BGR is approximately **50 miles/about one hour from Acadia**, versus approximately 161 miles/2.5–3 hours from PWM. [NPS directions](https://www.nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/directions.htm)
- A BGR round-trip rental supports the main goal: pick up and return at the same station.
- The penalty is the final flight: BGR→SEA requires a connection and Alaska does not serve BGR. Alaska status is most useful on the initial SEA→EWR flight, not the Maine legs.

### Portland version

Substitute **EWR→PWM nonstop**, rent round-trip at PWM, and return PWM→SEA with a connection. PWM offers more flights and may price lower, but it adds roughly **five to six hours of total Maine driving** across arrival and departure. It is worth choosing only if the combined airfare plus rental savings are substantial or Portland/Midcoast is itself part of the trip.

JFK is a workable second-choice gateway: Alaska flies SEA→JFK nonstop, Delta flies JFK→BGR nonstop, and several airlines serve JFK→PWM. The drawback is that the published JFK→BGR departures are generally morning or early afternoon, which makes the New York→Maine transfer consume more of the day than EWR's broader United schedule. [JFK–BGR schedule](https://www.directflights.com/JFK-BGR)

### Important services that remain open

- Park Loop Road and paved park roads are scheduled through December 1; Schoodic Loop Road is year-round.
- Carriage roads are generally year-round, subject to temporary weather and maintenance closures.
- Hulls Cove Visitor Center is scheduled through October 31.
- Jordan Pond House restaurant is scheduled through October 26; its store and carry-out run later in October.
- Cadillac Mountain vehicle reservations remain required through October 25, so reversing the trip does **not** avoid the reservation requirement. [Official reservation page](https://www.recreation.gov/timed-entry/400000)
- Blackwoods Campground remains available until October 19 at 10 a.m., covering nights through October 18 if reserved.

## How the reversal changes the trip

The park itself remains highly usable: roads, major trails, Cadillac access, Jordan Pond House, and Hulls Cove are open. The material change is **transportation and flexibility**, not loss of the headline scenery. Every full Acadia day occurs without Island Explorer, so parking strategy matters more and the rental car becomes indispensable. A carriage ride has only a two-day window, and several minor facilities close partway through the stay.

The upside is that the Acadia portion moves farther beyond the holiday weekend. Crowds may ease after October 12, while foliage should still be near its normal mid-October peak. Weather risk rises slightly as the visit moves later, and shorter daylight reduces recovery time after a slow start or full parking lot.

## Pros and cons

### Pros

- **Round-trip Maine rental:** pickup and return at BGR (or PWM), avoiding a one-way airport drop and simplifying pricing.
- **Alaska status used on the long outbound nonstop:** SEA→EWR is the preferred first search; SEA→JFK is the backup.
- **EWR works well as a through-line:** preferred NYC airport, Alaska service from Seattle, and United nonstop onward to Bangor.
- **Acadia after the holiday weekend:** potentially lower crowds than October 9–12 while remaining near typical peak foliage.
- **Five consecutive park days:** easier to move hikes around rain, fog, and wind.
- **No Maine→NYC rental-versus-flight decision at the end:** the trip finishes from the same Maine airport where the car was collected.

### Cons

- **It does not honestly fit the original window** with all eight full days; the clean version returns October 19.
- **No Island Explorer during the five full Acadia days:** more parking pressure, earlier starts, less flexibility for point-to-point hikes, and no car-free days.
- **Later seasonal closures:** both quiet-side campgrounds are closed, carriage rides have only a two-day window, and several minor facilities close during the stay.
- **No nonstop Maine→Seattle flight:** the final day requires a connection, and Alaska does not serve BGR or PWM.
- **Alaska status helps only part of the itinerary:** the NYC→Maine flight is United from EWR or Delta from JFK, and the return from Maine will be another carrier or partner itinerary.
- **Weather and daylight:** the latest Acadia dates have sunsets around 5:43 p.m.; wet leaves, granite, wind, or fog can reduce usable hiking time.
- **Compressed version is tiring:** preserving October 12 as a near-full NYC day forces a late flight, late rental pickup, and potentially a post-midnight arrival in Bar Harbor.

## Recommendation

Choose the reversed itinerary if the **round-trip rental, EWR preference, five consecutive weather-flexible park days, and post-holiday timing** outweigh the loss of shuttle service—and if returning October 19 is acceptable.

If October 18 is a hard return date, keep the same direction but describe October 12 honestly as a **mostly full NYC day**, not a full day. Use EWR→BGR rather than JFK→BGR because United's broader departure schedule gives the best chance of preserving that day. If car-free park access and more seasonal services matter more, retain the original Acadia-first itinerary.
