## Four-day framework

1. East-side coast: Sand Beach, Great Head or dry-weather Beehive, open Ocean Path sections, and Gorham Mountain.
2. Jordan Pond and central mountains: Jordan Pond Path, South Bubble from the pond, or dry-weather Pemetic.
3. West side and carriage roads: Beech Mountain alternatives, Southwest Harbor, Ship Harbor, and Bass Harbor Head Light.
4. Schoodic Peninsula: shoreline stops and short hikes; use October 7 to avoid the largest cruise day.

Cadillac Summit Road requires a separate vehicle reservation through October 25. Recheck NPS alerts for trail, road, and facility conditions before each outing.

## Walks, curiosities, and indoor stops

- **Bar Island land bridge:** walk from downtown Bar Harbor at low tide; the gravel bar is exposed for only 1.5 hours either side of low tide. Continue the short wooded trail for a view over town. Check the tide table first and leave as the tide rises; walking across is free, though a park entrance pass is required in season.
- **Bar Harbor Museum in the Streets:** self-guided historic walk among 26 downtown panels, with two longer village-perimeter routes. Panels are outdoors, so visit any time in daylight; the downtown circuit is easy to fit between meals or shops.
- **Abbe Museum (downtown):** Wabanaki-led museum focused on Wabanaki history and living culture. Listed hours: Monday–Saturday, 10 a.m.–5 p.m.; adult admission $18. The Sieur de Monts location is closed for maintenance.
- **Dorr Museum of Natural History:** student-designed natural-history exhibits at College of the Atlantic; fall hours listed as weekdays 10 a.m.–5 p.m. and Saturday 1–4 p.m.; admission by donation. Walkable from downtown on the Route 3 multi-use path; Island Explorer Route 2 stops at the campus.
- **Oceanarium & Education Center:** lobster and Maine-coast exhibits plus marsh boardwalk; fall hours are Wednesday–Sunday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m., with admission charged. Island Explorer serves it during the season; confirm daily programming and the current schedule.
- **Asticou Azalea Garden:** Japanese-inspired garden and pond in Northeast Harbor; open daily dawn to dusk through November 1, with a suggested $5 donation. Pair it with the nearby village and harbor walk.
- **Thuya Garden:** woodland garden and historic lodge in Northeast Harbor; open daily dawn to dusk through October 13, with lodge hours 10 a.m.–5 p.m. and a suggested $5 donation. Parking is limited.
- **Somesville:** pause at the small harbor, historic bridge, and village setting on the way to the west side; a useful brief photo stop when driving Route 102.
- **Bass Harbor Head Light:** dramatic working lighthouse and rocky shoreline viewpoint; the grounds are a short walk from the parking area. Parking is very limited, and visitors cannot enter the tower; arrive early and follow posted safety signs.

These businesses and gardens operate seasonally. Confirm hours before driving, especially for October 12–14, when many attractions change to reduced schedules or close.
